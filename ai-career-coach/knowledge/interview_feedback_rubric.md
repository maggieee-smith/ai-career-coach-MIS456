# Interview Answer Evaluation Rubric

## STAR method (behavioral questions)
- Situation: brief context — where, when, what was at stake (about 10–15% of the answer).
- Task: the candidate's specific responsibility or goal.
- Action: what the candidate personally did — the longest part (about 50–60%). Uses "I", not only "we".
- Result: the measurable or concrete outcome, plus what was learned.
- Strong answers run about 1.5–2 minutes when spoken (roughly 200–300 words).

## Scoring dimensions (1–5 each)
1. Structure: Is the answer organized (STAR for behavioral; clear framework for technical/case)?
2. Relevance: Does it answer the question that was asked and connect to the role?
3. Specificity: Concrete details, numbers, names of tools, and the candidate's own actions.
4. Impact: A clear result, ideally quantified, plus reflection or learning.
5. Communication: Concise, confident, no excessive filler words ("um", "like", "you know"), professional tone.

## Score interpretation
- 4.5–5: interview-ready, would advance.
- 3.5–4.4: solid, minor polish needed.
- 2.5–3.4: shows potential but missing structure, specificity, or result.
- Below 2.5: needs significant rework; consider choosing a different example.

## Common weaknesses to flag
- Rambling or no clear ending.
- Using "we" throughout so the interviewer can't tell what the candidate did.
- No result or a vague result ("it went well").
- Negative talk about former teammates or employers.
- For "weakness" questions: a fake weakness ("I work too hard") or one with no improvement plan.
- For technical questions: jumping to an answer without explaining reasoning.

## Feedback style
- Always give at least one specific strength and one specific improvement.
- Offer a rewritten example sentence or improved opening the student can use.
- Be encouraging but honest.
