# Resume Guide for College Students

## Format and layout
- One page for students and new grads. Recruiters spend roughly 6–10 seconds on a first scan, so the most important information must be easy to find.
- Order for students: Header, Education, Experience, Leadership/Involvement, Projects, Skills. Put Education first until you have full-time experience.
- Use one clean, readable font (Calibri, Garamond, Arial, Times New Roman) at 10–12 pt. Margins 0.5–1 inch.
- No photos, graphics, tables, text boxes, or two-column layouts. Applicant tracking systems (ATS) often misread them.
- Save and submit as PDF unless the posting asks for Word.
- Header: name, phone, professional email, city/state, LinkedIn URL. No full street address needed.
- Dates right-aligned and in a consistent format (e.g., "May 2026" or "05/2026", not both).

## Education section
- University, degree, major(s), minor, expected graduation month and year.
- Include GPA if 3.0 or higher (many finance/consulting/accounting firms expect it listed; omitting it can be read as below 3.0).
- Relevant coursework only if it adds signal (e.g., "Database Management, Business Analytics, Generative AI for Business").
- Honors, scholarships, Dean's List.

## Bullet points
- Start every bullet with a strong action verb: Led, Built, Analyzed, Automated, Negotiated, Designed, Reduced, Increased, Tested, Coordinated, Developed, Presented.
- Avoid weak openers: "Responsible for", "Helped with", "Worked on", "Duties included".
- Formula: Action verb + what you did + how/tools + measurable result. Example: "Automated weekly budget reconciliation in Excel using Power Query, cutting processing time from 3 hours to 20 minutes."
- Quantify wherever possible: dollars, percentages, number of people, time saved, volume handled. If an exact number isn't known, a defensible estimate ("~50 members") is acceptable.
- 2–5 bullets per role; more for the most relevant roles.
- Each bullet one to two lines. No periods needed at the end, but be consistent.

## Tense
- Current roles: present tense (Lead, Manage, Analyze).
- Past roles and completed accomplishments: past tense (Led, Managed, Analyzed).
- Never mix tenses within the same role, except a completed accomplishment in a current role may be past tense.

## Wording and tone
- Never use first-person pronouns (I, me, my).
- Cut filler: "various", "several", "successfully", "in order to".
- Avoid buzzwords without evidence ("team player", "hard worker", "detail-oriented") — show it in bullets instead.
- Mirror keywords from the target job description (tools, skills, certifications) so the ATS and recruiter see the match. Do not keyword-stuff.

## Skills section
- Group by type: Technical (SQL, Python, Tableau, Power BI, Excel – pivot tables/XLOOKUP, Alteryx, SAP), Certifications (CPA track, Security+, AWS Cloud Practitioner, Google Analytics), Languages.
- Only list skills you can discuss in an interview.
- Soft skills generally do not belong in the skills list.

## Leadership and involvement
- Treat student organizations like jobs: title, organization, dates, and quantified bullets (budget managed, members led, events run, money raised).
- Greek life, clubs, athletics, and volunteer work show leadership and time management.

## Projects
- Especially valuable for technical roles. Name, tools used, what it does, and a result or link (GitHub, portfolio).

## Common mistakes
- Typos and inconsistent formatting (the #1 reason for rejection at screening).
- Listing duties instead of accomplishments.
- Generic resume for every application — tailor the top third and keywords to each role.
- Including high school information after sophomore year of college (unless highly distinctive).
- Unprofessional email address.
- Over two pages, or crammed text with 0.3-inch margins.

## Tailoring by field
- Consulting / Advisory: problem solving, client-facing work, analysis, leadership; quantified impact.
- Accounting / Audit: GPA, CPA eligibility (150 credit hours), Excel, attention to detail, internships at firms.
- Finance: GPA, financial modeling, valuation, Excel, Bloomberg/FactSet, investment club.
- Technology / Data: languages, frameworks, projects with GitHub links, cloud certifications, hackathons.
- Marketing / PR: campaigns, content metrics (engagement, reach, followers), Canva/Adobe, writing samples.
- Healthcare: clinical rotations, licenses/certifications (BLS, CNA), patient volume.
