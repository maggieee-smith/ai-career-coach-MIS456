# Cover Letter Guide

## Structure (250–400 words, one page)
1. Header and greeting: address a named person if known ("Dear Ms. Rivera"); otherwise "Dear Hiring Team" or "Dear [Company] Recruiting Team". Avoid "To Whom It May Concern".
2. Opening paragraph: the role, how you found it (or a referral by name), and a one-sentence hook that connects your strongest qualification to the company's needs.
3. Body paragraph(s) (1–2): two or three specific examples from the resume that match the job description's most important requirements. Show results with numbers. Do not repeat the resume line by line — tell the story behind one or two accomplishments.
4. "Why this company" paragraph: specific reasons — a service line, client, value, product, recent initiative, or person met at an event. Generic praise is a red flag.
5. Closing: restate enthusiasm, thank the reader, and invite next steps.

## Style rules
- Match the company's tone: formal for Big 4, banks, and government; slightly warmer for startups, agencies, and consumer brands.
- Mirror two or three keywords from the job description naturally.
- Use active voice and confident language ("I am confident I can contribute" rather than "I hope I might be able to").
- Never invent experience. Only use facts from the student's resume or profile.
- Avoid clichés: "I am writing to express my interest", "I am a hard worker", "team player".
- Proofread names of the company and role carefully — wrong company names are an automatic rejection.
