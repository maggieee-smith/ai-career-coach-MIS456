# Career Paths by Major and Interest Profile

## RIASEC interest model (Holland Codes, used by the U.S. Department of Labor's O*NET Interest Profiler)
- Realistic (Doers): hands-on, building, tools, physical or technical systems. Careers: engineering, IT infrastructure, skilled trades, nursing (partly).
- Investigative (Thinkers): analysis, research, solving complex problems. Careers: data science, software development, cybersecurity, research, financial analysis.
- Artistic (Creators): creativity, design, writing, self-expression. Careers: marketing creative, PR, UX/UI design, content strategy.
- Social (Helpers): helping, teaching, counseling, healthcare. Careers: nursing, HR, teaching, counseling, client service.
- Enterprising (Persuaders): leading, selling, influencing, business. Careers: consulting, sales, management, wealth management, entrepreneurship, PR.
- Conventional (Organizers): structure, data, accuracy, processes. Careers: accounting, audit, database administration, compliance, financial operations.

## Common majors and typical first roles
- Management Information Systems (MIS): technology consultant, IT/technology auditor (technology assurance), business/systems analyst, data analyst, cybersecurity analyst, database administrator, product analyst, ERP consultant.
- Finance: financial analyst (FP&A), investment banking analyst, credit analyst, wealth management advisor, corporate treasury, risk analyst, consulting.
- Accounting: audit associate, tax associate, advisory/risk consultant, forensic accountant, corporate accountant.
- Marketing: marketing analyst, brand assistant, digital marketing specialist, market research analyst, social media manager, sales.
- Communications / Journalism: PR specialist, communications coordinator, content strategist, social media specialist, corporate communications.
- Management / Business Administration: management consultant, operations analyst, HR specialist, project coordinator, sales.
- Economics: economic consultant, financial analyst, data analyst, policy analyst, consulting.
- Computer Science: software engineer, data engineer, machine learning engineer, cybersecurity engineer.
- Data Science / Statistics: data scientist, data analyst, actuarial analyst, business intelligence analyst.
- Psychology: HR specialist, recruiter, market research analyst, UX researcher, counseling (graduate degree).
- Nursing: registered nurse (new grad residency), specialty nursing, nurse informatics later in career.
- Engineering: design engineer, test engineer, systems engineer, technical consulting, project engineer.

## Motivations and how they map to careers
- High earnings potential: investment banking, software engineering, consulting, data science, wealth management.
- Work-life balance: corporate finance, HR, government roles, many in-house analyst roles.
- Helping people directly: nursing, HR, teaching, client-facing advisory.
- Creativity: marketing, PR, UX/design, content.
- Stability and structure: accounting, audit, government, healthcare.
- Variety and travel: consulting, audit (client sites), sales.
- Building things / technical mastery: software, engineering, data.

## Skills that are valuable across careers in an AI-shaped job market
- Data literacy (Excel, SQL, a BI tool like Tableau or Power BI).
- Working effectively with generative AI tools (prompting, verifying outputs, automating workflows).
- Communication: writing clearly and presenting to non-experts.
- Domain expertise plus technology — e.g., accounting + data analytics, marketing + analytics, finance + Python.
- Certifications that signal initiative: CPA, CISA, Security+, AWS Cloud Practitioner, Google Data Analytics, Salesforce Administrator.
